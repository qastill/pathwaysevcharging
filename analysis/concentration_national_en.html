03Is it inequitable — does supply follow advantage?

The concentration index (CI) ranks residents by a socio-economic variable (low → high) and reads how much of the supply the bottom x % receive. +1 = everything at the top of the ranking, 0 = proportional, −1 = everything at the bottom. Dots are estimates, bars are 95 % bootstrap bands (1,000 resamples of districts (kabupaten/kota)); filled dots do not cross zero. 07Where the inequality comes from

Theil's T of chargers per resident is additively split into inequality between groups and within them. A large between-share means the gap is mostly one group against another; a small one means the gap lives inside every group.

08Does the verdict survive subsetting?

The headline indices recomputed on subsets. If a sign flips, the finding depends on which districts (kabupaten/kota) are in the frame.

09What it would take to equalise

Chargers that would have to be added so that every district reaches today's average per 100,000 residents, holding existing chargers in place (water-filling). A planning yardstick, not a recommendation.

11Method, sources and limits

Glossary
Concentration index (CI)
C = 2·covw(supply per resident, population-weighted fractional rank) / mean. Equivalent to 1 − 2∫L(p)dp on the concentration curve. Positive = supply concentrated among residents high on the ranking.
Erreygers CI
Correction for bounded outcomes (a yes/no flag or a 0–100 % share) so the index stays comparable across prevalence levels: 8·covw(y, R)/(max−min).
95 % band
Percentile bootstrap over districts (kabupaten/kota) (1,000 resamples). "Significant" = band excludes zero.
Gini (per resident)
Inequality of chargers per resident across districts (kabupaten/kota), weighted by residents; blind to who is rich or poor.
HHI / Neff / CRk
Herfindahl index of supply shares (0–10,000), its inverse (how many equal-sized districts (kabupaten/kota) would give the same concentration), and the share held by the top k districts (kabupaten/kota).
Theil T
Entropy inequality measure that decomposes exactly into between- and within-group parts.
20:20, Palma
Share of chargers held by the top 20 % of residents (ranked by chargers per resident) over the bottom 20 %; Palma = top 10 % over bottom 40 %.
